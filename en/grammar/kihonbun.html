Chapter 2Basic Sentences

This chapter explains the most basic sentence forms in Ruwish. After introducing the pronouns, we look at two kinds of sentences, copular sentences and existential sentences, and at how to negate each of them.

1. Pronouns

The Ruwish pronouns are as follows.

onetwo or more
First person我 / 私我們 / 私們
Second person尓尓輩
Third person他 (他男, 他女)他輩 (他男輩, 他女輩)
Non-human things斯斯輩

Words for oneself

我 and 私 both mean “I”. 私 is a word for deliberately making oneself small. It is used when the speaker wants to make the relationship with the listener clear, or to admonish oneself. Which one to use is not a grammatical rule; the speaker chooses in each situation.

Words for the listener

尓 is the basic word for “you”.

Words for other people

他 means “he” or “she”. To show gender, use 他男 (he) or 他女 (she). It is common to write 他男 or 他女 only the first time, and simply 他 after that.

故because
他女she
疲，tired,
他she
眠sleep

“She was tired, so she went to sleep.”

Words for non-human things

To refer to something that is not a person, use 斯 (this, that) instead of 他.

Words for plurals

To show two or more, Ruwish uses two words, 們 and 輩. 們 makes a plural that includes the speaker (我們 = we), and 輩 makes a plural that does not (尓輩 = you all, 他輩 = they).

2. Copular sentences

Sentences that state what the subject is or what it is like, such as “A is B” or “A is ...”, are called copular sentences. In copular sentences, three copular verbs are used for different purposes.

Sentence typeCopular verbMeaningWhat follows
Equative sentence何A is Bnoun
Naming sentence称A is called Bnoun
Adjectival sentence很A is ...adjective

Equative sentences

To state what the subject is, use 何.

“This is an apple.”

Naming sentences

To state a name, use 称.

“My name is RUI.”

To negate it, place 不 before 称. A sentence with 称 negated means “it is not called an apple”, in other words, “it is not an apple”.

“This is not called an apple. (This is not an apple.)”

3. Existential sentences

To state that something exists, use 在 or 見. Where it is goes after the verb.

三three
檎果apple
在exist
机table
乗top

“There are three apples on the table.”

見 is used when the speaker has seen and confirmed it with their own eyes. 在 is used to state that something exists without having seen it.

三three
檎果apple
見exist (seen)
机table
乗top

“There are three apples on the table (I can see them).”

Negation

To say “there is not a single one”, place 那 (any) before the subject, and use 不在.

那any
檎果apple
不not
在exist

“There are no apples at all.”
